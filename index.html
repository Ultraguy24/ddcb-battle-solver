▸ HOW THE BATTLE FLOW WORKS

Turns are sequential, not simultaneous. One player runs their whole turn (Entrance if they have no active → DP Phase → digivolve check), then the turn passes to the other player. The Attack Phase only happens if both sides currently have an active — otherwise it's skipped and play passes on.

Kickoff — tell the tool who won the random first-turn draw. This matters a lot: the turn player attacks first by default every bout, and also gets to commit their support card second, reacting to what the non-turn player already revealed.

Entrance — pick the active Digimon for whichever side's turn it is. Entering directly with a Champion or Ultimate (instead of digivolving up to it later) automatically halves that card's HP and all three attacks — the tool applies this for you.

DP Phase — the turn player may sacrifice one hand card for DP, or digivolve using already-banked DP, but never both in the same turn. DP totals persist across the whole duel.

Attack Phase — both sides pick an attack blind. Then support is revealed in a fixed order: the non-turn player commits first, and the turn player reacts second with full knowledge of that. The tool splits this into the right steps depending on whose turn it is, and ranks your hand's support options (including "none") by worst-case outcome at each step.

Opponent's hand is visible in this game, so track it in the "Opponent's Hand" box just like your own. This upgrades the non-turn-player support commitment from a guess into a true worst-case calculation: instead of padding their damage with an assumed buffer, the tool checks every attack × every real support card actually in their tracked hand and finds the genuine worst case. Keep it accurate — an empty tracked hand is treated as "they have nothing," which will overstate how safe a pick is if you just haven't logged their cards yet.

Sacrifice/digivolve are not mutually exclusive — you can do both in the same DP phase. The Sacrifice Advisor accounts for this: sacrificing a specific card gets a large bonus if doing so would immediately unlock a digivolve target already sitting in your hand this same turn.

Damage resolution: the turn player's attack lands first by default. If it KOs the opponent, their attack never happens. A card's own "1st attack" trait (or an unconditional "Attack first" support effect) only matters for the non-turn player — it's how they steal that order away; it does nothing for the turn player, who already had priority.

What's auto-modeled: base attack values; "<Specialty> foe x3"-style Cross bonuses; "O/T/X to 0" negation; turn-order-based first strike (with steal-back via "1st attack" traits); "force both players to attack X" support effects; and common Option/support numeric effects (flat boosts, attack-specific boosts, attack doubling, attack zeroing).
What's shown as text only: discards, HP swaps, specialty swaps, "jamming," "counter" ordering effects, and anything conditional on hidden information — these get flagged so you can factor them in yourself.
